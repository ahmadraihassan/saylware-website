# Who to talk to

Saylware sells two things operators actually feel:

1. **Cybersecurity** — MDR, incident response, vulnerability management,
   advisory. Humans filter noise. Leadership gets a readable report.
2. **Customer care** — branded helpdesk, CX process, escalation/QA,
   support analytics. Agents sound like the company, not a call center.

Your site already shows the businesses that fit: pest control, auto shops,
physical security firms, and IT/ops teams. Stay in that world. Do not
pitch Google, banks, or companies with a 40-person SOC.

## Best-fit (write these first)

| Signal | Why it fits | Offer to lead with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner-operated service company in El Paso / West Texas / NM | You share a 915 number. Local is a reason to pick up. | Customer care overflow + Texas SB 2610 advisory |
| Dispatch / 24-7 / emergency language on their site | Phones and tickets pile up on the owner. | Managed desk + escalation/QA |
| Hiring CS, office, or “route coordinator” roles | Volume outgrew the current desk. | Desk or CX process design |
| 20–200 employees, no security team on LinkedIn | They hold customer PII, card data, or cameras. | Advisory first, MDR if they are ready |
| Early SaaS (UAE / Pakistan / UK) hiring support | They need a desk before they can hire a team. | CX process + branded desk |
| Physical security / alarm / fire contractors | They already sell “someone always answers.” | After-install support desk, plus cyber for their own stack |

## Who to skip (for now)

- National franchises with a corporate contact center (Orkin HQ, etc.).
  A local franchise owner can be a fit; the brand’s 800-number is not.
- Direct competitors (local MSPs selling the same SOC). Partner later,
  do not cold-pitch against them.
- Anyone who only has a consumer Gmail and no company page.
- Enterprises with a named CISO and a published SOC vendor.

## Titles that actually reply

Write to **one** person, in this order:

1. Owner / President / Founder
2. VP Ops / General Manager
3. Head of Support / Office Manager / Dispatch
4. IT Manager (security track only)

Do not write the intern, the marketing coordinator, or five people at
once.

## Two tracks, one conversation

You do **not** pitch both services in the first note. Pick the door that
matches what you saw:

- Phones, reviews, hiring, “we answer,” emergency hours → **care**
- Cameras, customer data, card payments, Texas law, no IT hire → **security**

If they engage, the form on saylware.com already routes Care → Security
→ General. Let the conversation do that, not the first sentence.

## Texas hook (use carefully)

Texas SB 2610 (effective 1 Sep 2025) gives businesses under 250 employees
a **safe harbor from exemplary damages** after a breach *if* they can
show a documented cybersecurity program scaled to their size. It is
voluntary. It is not a scare law.

Use it as a useful fact for Texas shops. Never as “you will be fined
tomorrow.” Never as a fake audit.
