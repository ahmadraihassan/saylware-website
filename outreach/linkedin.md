# LinkedIn (primary channel)

LinkedIn is where this stays human. Your domain is not involved until
someone wants a longer note.

Company page: [linkedin.com/company/saylware](https://www.linkedin.com/company/saylware/)

## Profile (do this before any outreach)

Paste `assets/linkedin-profile.md`. Then:

- Photo: real face, not a logo. Neutral background.
- Banner: optional. If you use one, keep it charcoal + amber, no slogans.
- Featured: `https://saylware.com` and one service page
  (`/services/managed-support-desk` or `/services/managed-detection-response`).
- Experience: Saylware, current, with the same one-line description as
  the headline.
- Open to: **not** “open to work.” You are selling a service.

Connect your personal profile to the company page as an employee.

## Daily cadence (Mon–Fri)

| When | What | Cap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morning, 20 min | 8–12 connection notes from the current batch | Hard cap |
| Midday, 15 min | Reply to anyone who accepted. Send the DM in the brief. | Only acceptances |
| Once | One useful comment on *their* post, if they posted | Optional |
| Friday | One short company or personal post (see below) | 1 |

Do not automate connection requests. LinkedIn notices, and it reads as
spam even when the text is nice.

## Connection note (300 characters, so keep it shorter)

Pattern:

1. Their name / their company, one concrete detail
2. Why you are not a stranger (El Paso / same kind of operator / a
   specific page on their site)
3. No pitch. No link. No “I’d love to pick your brain.”

The briefs in `leads/batch-01.md` already fit this length. If you write
a new one, read it out loud. If you would not say it at a coffee shop,
cut it.

## First DM (after they accept, wait a few hours)

Pattern:

1. Thanks for connecting — one beat, not a paragraph
2. The observation (the same fact as the connection note, slightly deeper)
3. What Saylware actually does, in one sentence, matched to *their* pain
4. A small ask: “open to a 15-minute call next week, or is this a miss?”

Never send a PDF. Never send a Calendly on first DM unless they asked
for times.

## Follow-up (once)

Wait 5–7 days. If they accepted but did not reply:

> Still thinking about the [specific thing]. If a short call is useful I
> am around. If not, I will leave you in peace.

Then stop. Move them to `closed_no` in the tracker.

## Posts that attract inbound (weekly)

Write as an operator, not a vendor. 80–120 words. No hashtag walls.

Examples you can rotate:

- What a noisy alert queue actually does to a 12-person shop
- Why “outsourced support” fails when agents do not get the brand voice
- Texas SB 2610 in plain language: what a 25-person company actually
  needs to document (not a fear post)
- A dispatch story: the ticket that sat because nobody owned the
  escalation

End with a question, not “DM me for a demo.”
